# SCI vs LMNP France: Choosing Your Property Structure

# SCI vs LMNP France: Choosing Your Property Structure

Buying property in France? Don't get caught out by tax pitfalls – SCI or LMNP, which structure is right for you?

France offers a compelling property market for international buyers, but navigating its complexities, especially regarding ownership structures and taxation, can be a significant challenge for expats. Two common frameworks for holding and managing French real estate are the Société Civile Immobilière (SCI) and the Loueur en Meublé Non Professionnel (LMNP) status. Both offer distinct advantages and disadvantages, and the choice between them often leads to confusion for newcomers. This article will clarify the differences between SCI and LMNP, helping you make an informed decision to optimize your French property investment and build long-term wealth.

## What is a SCI?

A Société Civile Immobilière (SCI) is a civil company structure commonly used in France for owning and managing property, particularly for shared ownership among family members or for estate planning purposes. It allows multiple individuals to collectively own a property, with each owner holding shares proportional to their investment. While not a commercial entity by default, a SCI can opt for corporate tax (Impôt sur les Sociétés - IS) or remain tax transparent, being taxed at the individual level (Impôt sur le Revenu - IR).

**Taxation of SCI:**
*   **Impôt sur le Revenu (IR):** Under this regime, the SCI is tax-transparent. Profits are attributed to the partners in proportion to their shareholding and declared on their personal income tax returns as rental income (revenus fonciers). Deductible expenses include loan interest, renovation costs, property tax, insurance, and general company charges. Partners benefit from the individual capital gains tax rules, with full exemption after 22 years of ownership.
*   **Impôt sur les Sociétés (IS):** If the SCI opts for corporate tax, it is taxed on its profits. A reduced rate of 15% applies to profits up to €42,500 if the share capital is held at least 75% by individuals and the turnover is below €10 million. Profits exceeding this threshold are taxed at the standard corporate rate of 25%. When profits are distributed to partners, they are treated as dividends and taxed personally. Capital gains are taxed at a flat professional rate of 25% with no holding period abatements.

**Pros and Cons of SCI for Expats:**
*   **Pros:** Flexibility in property management, facilitates inheritance and succession planning, allows for shared ownership with clear governance.
*   **Cons:** Can involve more administrative complexity and costs compared to direct ownership, IS taxation can be less favourable for capital gains compared to IR, potential for increased scrutiny under recent legislation like the Loi de finances pour 2026.

## What is LMNP?

LMNP (Loueur en Meublé Non Professionnel) is not a company structure but a tax status for individuals who rent out furnished properties in France. It is designed for non-professional landlords who earn rental income from furnished accommodations. The primary appeal of the LMNP status lies in its significant tax advantages, particularly the ability to offset rental income with a depreciation (amortissement) of the property and its furnishings.

**Tax Benefits of LMNP:**
*   **Amortization:** This is the core benefit. You can depreciate the value of the property and the furniture over time. This depreciation is a non-cash expense that can be deducted from your rental income, often reducing your taxable income to zero.
*   **Tax Regimes:**
    *   **Micro-BIC:** A simplified regime where 50% of your turnover (up to certain thresholds) is automatically deducted for expenses. This is generally less advantageous than the *régime réel* for furnished rentals.
    *   **Régime Réel:** Under this regime, you can deduct actual expenses, including mortgage interest, property taxes, insurance, repairs, and importantly, the amortization of the property and its contents. This typically offers the greatest tax efficiency.

**Key Considerations for LMNP:**
*   **Income Tax and Social Contributions:** While LMNP offers tax benefits, the net income (after deductions and amortization) is subject to income tax and social security contributions (prélèvements sociaux).
*   **Furnished Rentals Only:** This status strictly applies to furnished property rentals.

## Head-to-Head: SCI vs. LMNP for Expats

Choosing between a SCI and LMNP involves weighing several factors against your specific investment goals:

*   **Tax Efficiency:** For pure rental income generation from furnished properties, LMNP with the *régime réel* often provides superior tax efficiency due to amortization, potentially leading to zero taxable income for many years. A SCI taxed under IR offers tax transparency but lacks the amortization benefit. A SCI taxed under IS can be efficient for reinvesting profits within the company but can be less advantageous for distributions or capital gains compared to LMNP or direct ownership.
*   **Administrative Burden:** Setting up and managing a SCI involves more administrative steps, including company registration, annual general meetings, and distinct accounting. LMNP status, when managed directly by an individual, generally has a lower administrative burden, though professional accounting is recommended for the *régime réel*.
*   **Flexibility:** LMNP is tied to individual ownership and is best suited for individuals or couples. A SCI offers more flexibility for multiple owners, shared investment, and estate planning.
*   **Investment Goals:** If your primary goal is to generate regular, tax-optimised income from a furnished rental, LMNP is often the preferred route. If you are pooling resources with others, planning for inheritance, or seeking a structure for complex property management, a SCI might be more appropriate.
